Icon of the Seas isn't a cruise ship that happens to have amenities — it's a destination that happens to move. The Category 6 waterpark has six slides, including the tallest waterslide at sea. The Hideaway is an adults-only pool deck with a swim-up bar. Crown's Edge is a sky walk that hangs off the side of the ship, 150 feet above the water. The aqua theater puts on full productions over the ocean. Dinner options include a Japanese steakhouse, a seafood restaurant, a supper club, and Windjammer when you just want to pile a plate and sit down.

Icon of the Seas makes her one-time repositioning run from Miami to her new home in Galveston, and she's routing through Curaçao and Aruba to make the journey worth it. These aren't ports that show up on typical Galveston itineraries. Curaçao's Willemstad is one of the most photographed waterfronts in the Caribbean — Dutch colonial buildings in pinks, blues, and yellows reflected in the harbor. Aruba's Eagle Beach is consistently ranked among the best beaches in the world: flat water, white sand, no seaweed, negligible rain. You won't get this combination again from Texas.

Icon's first group of 8-night sailings from Galveston, and one of only six on the schedule. The Western Caribbean routing is the standard Texas itinerary — but on a ship that makes everything else on the Gulf feel like a warmup. Cozumel's reef system is one of the top dive destinations in the world; even a snorkel excursion puts you over coral formations that take a minute to process. Roatán's West Bay Beach is legitimately great — calm, clear, palm-lined, with easy access to the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef. Costa Maya is the quieter port day: a relaxed beach club environment, no cruise ship crowds spilling into a town, easy to unplug. CocoCay rounds it out.

What's included in both options

The Ocean View Balcony on Icon gives you a private outdoor space wide enough for two chairs and a small table — which matters most on sea days and port approaches. The balcony faces outward, so you get full horizon views rather than the interior atrium. Beds convert between king and twins on request. Midship placement on the main cabin decks keeps motion minimal and the walk to everything short.
